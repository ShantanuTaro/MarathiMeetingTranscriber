      How it works

      1

      Pick a service, add a key

      Nothing is stored on this server between runs. You paste an API key, it lives in memory for the session, and it is gone the moment you end it.

      2

      Upload and transcribe

      The recording is uploaded and transcribed by the service you picked. Anything longer than its per-file limit is cut into equal parts, sent one at a time and stitched back onto a single clock.

      3

      Flag the doubts

      Where the service reports per-word confidence, the weak words are highlighted in the document and collected into a review table at the top.

      Reading the document

      The transcript is a draft with its uncertainty marked, not a finished record.

      Every highlighted word also appears in a table at the top of the document with its timestamp and a blank Correction column, so a reviewer works down a checklist rather than reading the whole hour.

      • Yellow. The model scored this word below the confidence threshold. Jump to the timestamp and check it.
      • Turquoise. An English word that came out in Latin script instead of Devanagari. The model decides this per word and is not consistent about it, so these are flagged for a human rather than transliterated automatically.

      FAQ

      Where does my recording go?

      To the transcription service you selected, and nowhere else. This server keeps the upload only until the transcript is written, then deletes it, but the copy the service received is not this server’s to delete. Read their retention policy before sending anything sensitive.

      Where is my API key kept?

      In this server’s memory, for the length of the session. It is never written to disk, never sent back to the page, and never included in any status response. Ending the session, or restarting the server, erases it. Closing the tab does not: use the End session button.

      Why does the progress bar creep instead of stepping?

      Because none of these services report progress. They accept a file and answer when they are finished. The bar is an estimate from how long the job has been running against the length of the recording, shown with a “~” because that is a guess, not a measurement. It eases toward the end and waits there until the transcript actually lands.

      What does it cost?

      Sarvam bills ₹30 per hour of audio, or ₹45 with speaker labels on, counted per second from the moment a job starts; a run that fails or is stopped is billed in full. ElevenLabs bills against your plan’s quota. The meter above the drop zone counts what this session has spent.

      Can I upload several recordings at once?

      Yes. They queue and run one at a time, so a rate limit on your key stops one job rather than all of them.

      Can I treat the transcript as a record?

      No. It is an unverified machine-generated draft: speech recognition makes mistakes and makes them confidently, and speaker labels are statistical guesses. Read it and correct it before it is used for anything that matters, and see the terms and disclaimer.

      Nothing is highlighted. Is it perfect?

      Unlikely. On Sarvam nothing is ever highlighted, since it reports no per-word confidence, so the check never runs. Elsewhere it means the threshold is set too low for this audio. Treat an empty review table as a reason to spot-check, never as a clean bill of health.
